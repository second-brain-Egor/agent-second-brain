## @kyleplaysgu1tar

“Graphic design, KILLED.” “Video editing, DESTROYED!” “3D MODELING, done for.” I see more and more of these click bait titles every day. Why is the seeming death or elimination of meaningful human creative acts seems to be celebrated? What the hell kind of life do y’all want for yourselves? Life is in the doing. Work is not just a Sisyphean task to be eliminated, it’s an expression of our dignity, our capacity, our desire to participate in communal work with others toward a common good. All work, from janitors to office workers to anything, has dignity, purpose, and meaning. 

Work is a form of self donation, a means of self communication and can be actually a form of love. It is a way of contributing ourselves to a wider whole. We are made to work by the sweat of our brow. 

Acedia, an ancient concept recognized by philosophers and monks, thousands of years ago is a vice characterized by a sadness at the good. “I abhor what is, and I crave what is not.” Often it would manifest amongst those who, weary of the work in front of them, monks would turn from their work and look at the sun wondering how much time has passed. It is impatience at our limits, a frustration at our very nature, of the necessary diligence and methodical nature of the work in front of us , and a desire for it to come to an end quicker than is naturally possible. It is a sadness of the real, at hatred of being, of participatory action in a world that demands it from us, and a world that we are inherently in relationship with, and with people, who we are inherently in relationship with. It is a dread of the prospect of a cooperation with others, and with the world, to produce. It is caused by an inward idolization of the self. It says “I’d rather not.” AI is the latest supreme manifestation of this. In AI, we can finally relax from the execution of work and relish in the delegation of tasks to autonomous agents. We become our own little kings in lonely, isolated kingdoms. We relieve ourselves of the natural friction that comes with cooperating with other people towards a common goal. We free ourselves of the virtue built, the mutual interdependence/trust gained, and the humanity expressed, necessary among people of diverse skillsets and gifts, friendships produced, to make something together. For what? So we can concentrate more of the value extracted from labor to ourselves instead of the unhappy reality of having to share and distribute to those deserving and qualified. It is the very infrastructure of selfishness and greed, which fuel their reign and hatred of cooperative work and shared benefit, of the necessary friction yet unparalleled reward and connection of working with others toward a common goal, won by the sweat of the brow and the successful coordination of individuals humble enough to deal with one another cooperatively and charitably. It is the antithesis of human unity and communion. 

All this to say, I am not condemning AI itself as a tool, but rather those that promote it and use it as a careless and ridiculous celebration of death to human living, doing, and loving, and promoting an image of humanity as one that’s achieved fulfillment in an escape from work or being an active participant in the world. Instead of seeing it as a subordinate tool, people promote it as an intellectual guillotine, a means of self transcendence by decapitating the body from the mind. 

With that, good day, and remember to touch grass look people in the eye and say hello to your neighbors.

## @TheDandonian

It's not about getting rid of work, it's about getting rid of blockers that prevented you from creating. Video games, movies, TV series, etc, have been exclusive domain for the wealthy and the fortunate. 

If you want to act in a movie but you can't convince someone to give you a shot, you're screwed. If you have a great idea for a movie but you can't afford to pay anyone to be in it, you can't afford the equipment or the set or special effects, you're out of luck. If you have a brilliant idea for a video game but you can't afford to pay hundreds of highly skilled people, you're very limited in what you can achieve. If you've written a beautiful song but you can't sing, or if you can sing but you don't have a band... or you can play music but you don't have a singer... 

The point of AI isn't to kill creation, it's to empower actual creators. Think about everything you hate about hollywood and the games industry, where the sequel factories are shoving the same crap out every year and the publishers who force developers to cut features, or launch when the game isn't ready. Think about the music industry where someone like Taylor Swift had to rerecord all of her music because the master versions were sold without her consent. Every movie, album, TV series and video game you've ever loved, was a prime example of creators having to sign away rights to their creations in exchange for the finances to create it. That's what a record deal is. 

 AI gets rid of all these issues because it puts the power in the hands of the creator. It also speeds up the process. If you want to write a song about today, you can release it tonight in full studio quality, with your own voice on the track. If you want to make a TV series where you're the lead actor, that's entirely possible now. The creative world is truly your oyster and no longer exclusively available for the super rich. It also means we can do bigger and better things than ever. The cost to create is pretty much free, so there's no pressure to hit gigantic financial targets. You don't have to compromise by putting a close up of a car in your movie just to get the sponsorship money. You don't have to let the rich have a cut of your earnings. You aren't restricted by the tools you can afford because you build the tools you need.
